Who I Am

I’m a sophomore at Dartmouth College pursuing a degree in Quantitative Social Science with a concentration in Environmental Governance, alongside minors in Government and Chemistry. Selected Work

Research Data Analysis
Are We Feeling the Heat? Disaster Exposure and Climate Beliefs Across U.S. Counties
QSS 20 — Dartmouth College, August 2026

A county-level analysis of 3,144 U.S. counties testing whether exposure to federally declared natural disasters predicts support for regulating carbon dioxide and belief that global warming is affecting specific hazards. Merges FEMA disaster declarations, Yale Climate Opinion Maps, and MIT Election Lab returns in Python. Finds that partisanship explains most of the variation in county climate opinion, while hazard-specific disaster experience shifts beliefs only at the margin.

Research Environmental Chemistry
PCBs in the Hudson: Pollution, Contamination, and Billions Spent
Chem 63 — Dartmouth College, August 2026

An analysis of the regulation, litigation, and scientific methods surrounding General Electric’s dumping of PCBs into the Hudson River. Traces the path from the EPA’s 1984 “no action” decision to a $1.7 billion dredging cleanup, and compares two studies of PCBs in Hudson River fish published nearly 20 years apart to show how the science kept arriving after the policy decisions it should have informed.

Policy Op-Ed
The Solar Savings Act: Doubling New York’s Solar Tax Credit
Govt 30.16: Politics of Climate Change — Dartmouth College, March 2026

A narrative op-ed and supporting policy brief proposing legislation to double New York State’s solar panel tax credit (25% → 50%, capped at $10,000), funded by a modest cannabis excise tax increase. Addresses the gap left by federal credit cuts and argues for bipartisan appeal through the lens of energy independence and financial pragmatism.
